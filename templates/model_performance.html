Dataset Transparency & Academic Integrity

Handling of the Salary Attribute

The publicly available Medical Cost Personal Dataset (Kaggle) originally contains 7 columns: age, sex, bmi, children, smoker, region, charges. It does not natively include an individual income or salary field.

To support insurance affordability analysis, premium-to-income ratios, and financial suitability scoring within this MCA final-year project, a documented academic proxy is generated deterministically (180000 + age*6500 + bmi*2500 + children*12000). Crucially, the training pipeline and production inference pipeline use the exact same feature schema (7 features) to prevent covariate mismatch or untrained feature leakage.

Viva Presentation Guide

Why Accuracy Percentage is Not Used for Regression

MAE (Mean Absolute Error)

The average absolute magnitude of errors between predicted and actual charges. Preserves original INR currency units and provides direct financial interpretability.

RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)

Square root of mean squared deviations. Penalizes large prediction errors more severely than MAE, ensuring no extreme catastrophic mispredictions.

R² Score (Variance Explained)

Quantifies the proportion of total variance in medical charges explained by the model's independent variables. A score of ~0.87 means 87% of cost variation is captured.

Note on unsupported accuracy claims: In statistical machine learning, continuous regression tasks cannot claim a single percentage "accuracy" (e.g. "95% accuracy") because predictions are real-valued numbers, not discrete binary classes. Performance is rigorously evaluated via MAE, RMSE, and R² Score.
